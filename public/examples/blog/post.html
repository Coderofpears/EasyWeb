The long way around is sometimes the shortcut.

On patient systems, useful detours, and the quiet compounding power of returning to a question.

Written by Mira ChenAugust 25, 2026

There is a particular kind of progress that looks, from a distance, like standing still. A sketch revisited. A sentence rewritten. A product opened again with one small question in mind.

We tend to celebrate the straight line because it is easy to explain. But the work that lasts is often made through loops: make a version, live with it, notice what keeps catching, and try again with more information than before.

“The detour is not a delay when it changes the destination.”

A good system leaves a little room for this. It makes the next useful action obvious without pretending that every answer is known. It keeps history close enough to learn from and the future open enough to surprise you.

Return with better questions

That may be the simplest definition of craft: not moving quickly in one direction, but returning with better questions. The long way around is only long if we measure the distance and ignore the view.
